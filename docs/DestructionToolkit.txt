Game Blueprint: /Game/Crusader/Destruction/B_CRDestructible

The traversal map contains a destruction range west of the surface firing range,
centred at (-4300, -6650, 0). Nineteen targets cover fourteen geometry collections:
concrete pillars, wood/plaster walls, exposed beams, framed windows, furniture,
mugs and a vase. Four of these targets form the grenade bay. Rifle, pistol
and shotgun pickups are at the entrance. E picks up; LMB fires; hold/release H
throws; X selects grenade/smoke. Restart Play to restore the targets.

CRDestructibleActor is the native parent of a project-owned copy of the toolkit
breakable Blueprint. Committed authoritative Lyra hits, including guard weapons
and robot mounted weapons, invoke the toolkit's BulletImpact interface. Existing
laser trails, weapon sounds and surface impacts stay in the Crusader effects
system. Each shotgun blast applies one physics field per struck destructible,
while preserving individual pellet impacts. The toolkit supplies fracture
geometry, break sounds and debris effects.
Physical material identities are retained for Chaos Niagara; their surface IDs
are mapped to Crusader's existing surface table.

Grenades apply bounded external strain to nearby destructibles with a cover
trace; smoke does not. Chaos particle state replicates from the server. A small
replicated break-effect sample also drives remote material changes and effects,
because replicated particle motion does not consistently emit break delegates.
Loose pieces ignore Pawn and Camera collision. Bullet-hole decals are disabled
on geometry collections because they cannot follow individual moving fragments.
Optional static frames still accept normal surface impacts.
These test fixtures do not export their dense fracture meshes to navigation;
they are placed outside the existing crowd patrol district.

Authoring: Configure-Destruction.py, then Build-DestructionRange.py through
Send-Editor.py with PIE stopped. The range builder updates only its own actors.
Gameplay checks: Test-DestructionSuite.py. Persisted validation:
Validate-Destruction.py and resources/DestructionValidation.json.

Destructible furniture in the two existing buildings

Build-BuildingDestruction.py converts 27 selected placements (11 prop types, with
17 geometry collections for their different sizes) from the imported environment
models. Placement scale is baked into geometry, bone translations and convex
collision; runtime collection components use unit scale. This keeps Chaos query
shapes aligned with the visible furniture. The facility includes its office
chair, desk with monitor/keyboard/terminal, drawer with storage box, laboratory
supplies, and cargo crates. The control room includes terminals, equipment cases,
vault crates, cargo boxes and barrels. These are the original sci-fi meshes and
outside texture materials with a dark metal material on newly exposed cut faces.
Walls, roofs and the large command consoles remain intact. Barrels break as props;
they do not produce an extra explosion or radiation damage.

The furniture Blueprint is /Game/Crusader/Destruction/Furniture/B_CRDestructibleFurniture.
Its toolkit data assets provide metal sounds and sparks. Collision damage is disabled
so settling or brushing against a chair does not shatter it. Bullets and grenades
still break it. Intact clusters block characters and cameras; released pieces ignore
them and shrink away after resting for 12-18 seconds. A lightweight navigation modifier
excludes the intact bounds and is removed on the first break. UCRDestructibleNavigation
exports that box directly because collections lack the ordinary static-mesh collision
body setup required by Unreal's standard NavModifierComponent. Dense fracture
triangles are never exported to the navigation mesh.
The traversal navmesh uses Dynamic generation so affected tiles can rebuild and
restore walkable floor after a broken footprint is removed.
Breaking-event filtering is disabled: even a stationary or lightweight piece must
notify gameplay so its navigation obstacle and replicated break state update.
Collision/trailing filters and the existing sound/network throttles remain active.
Furniture also applies contact strain to the bone reported by the weapon hit. This
keeps tabletop/edge hits effective even when the center of mass lies outside the
toolkit's radial impact field; contact strain affects only the struck collection.

Desk accessories and stacked boxes belong to their support's collection, so they
fall with it. Original StaticMeshActors are retained in the Editor Sources folder,
hidden, without collision, and marked editor-only (excluded from cooked games).
They preserve transforms/materials and remain compatible with the existing building
generators. Both generators reapply the furniture pass when its manifest exists.
The furniture pass alone updates no building walls, roofs, posts or other props.
Assets and source transforms are recorded in resources/BuildingDestruction.json.

Run the furniture builder with Play stopped. Set BD_REBUILD_COLLECTIONS=True before
executing it only when intentionally refracturing; normally it reuses matching assets.
CRDestructionTools uses Unreal's Fracture/PlanarCut editor modules, which are not required
at runtime. GetRigidPieceCount also supports validation of the generated assets.
Test-BuildingDestructionSuite.py exercises placed objects with real weapon/throw input
in standalone and two-player PIE. Validate-BuildingDestruction.py verifies the saved
placements, original material/bounds preservation, collision and gameplay reports.
Restart Play to restore destroyed furniture.
