Mechanical robot rigs
====================

Six robots: IronSentinel, SentinelWalker, APP5200IndustrialM, IroncladTitan,
IroncladVanguard and IroncladSentinel.

Open in Unreal
--------------
Content/Crusader/Robots/<Name> contains the skeletal mesh, its custom skeleton,
PBR materials, Textures, the four-second <Name>_JointCheck animation and
BP_<Name>_Preview. Drag the preview Blueprint into a level and press Play to
exercise both legs. Preview actors have no collision or AI controller.

The Parts subfolder contains independently imported static modules. These use
bone-local pivots; RigManifest.json maps each part to its attachment bone.
They are alternatives to sections of the complete skeletal mesh, so attaching
them on top of that mesh would duplicate the geometry.

The rigs have chassis, upper_leg, middle_leg, lower_leg and foot bones. Each
metal piece has exactly one bone influence at full weight. Separate joint
housings, axle inserts and closed cut rims allow the pieces to rotate without
stretching their surfaces. The four piston-equipped models also have separate
housing and rod bones. Upper body and weapons remain a rigid chassis assembly.

These are custom mechanical skeletons. They do not use the humanoid mannequin
skeleton or the existing humanoid visual override/locomotion system. JointCheck
is a rig inspection animation. Gameplay uses a separate native mechanical gait
on CRRobotCharacter; it does not loop JointCheck as a walk animation.

Gameplay in L_TraversalGym
-------------------------
Six B_Robot_<Name> pawns patrol with the existing human crowds: four start in
the outdoor district and two in the facility checkpoint/hall. They share the
crowd controller's Detour avoidance, linked tether areas, sight/hearing,
investigation, pursuit and tactical firing positions. Actors are labeled
CR_Robot_<Name> under the crowd Robots folders in the level outliner.

Robots only retaliate against the actor that damages them or fires a directed
near miss within their capsule radius plus 55 cm. Seeing a player or hearing
ambient gunfire can cause investigation but never grants permission to shoot.
Other guards/robots are friendly. Unrelated noise cannot replace an aggressor.
After losing the attacker for 18 seconds they disengage and resume patrol.

Mounted guns fire three-shot laser bursts with modest spread and shared Laser
Tracer 5, muzzle, impact and sound effects. Damage is server authoritative.
World cover and intervening pawn space block fire. Muzzle points are authored
per model and follow chassis pitch/recoil. Existing humanoid weapons retain
their original effects/audio path.

The native animation instance plants feet using ground traces, solves only
the mechanical hinge axes, and keeps every rigid link at unit scale. Stride
length follows leg length; feet adjust to changes of direction. Landings use
the project's surface footstep profile. Normal navigation and hops are
supported. Humanoid slide, vault and climb montages are deliberately excluded
from these nonhumanoid skeletons; robot-specific parkour is not implemented.

Each robot uses one capsule for movement and weapon hits (no per-part damage).
On death it stops shooting and lowers into a mechanical shutdown pose, then
respawns after the death lifecycle plus five seconds with the same model,
armor and home area. It does not use the humanoid ragdoll/get-up system.

resources/RobotGameplay.json contains initial placement, armor and barrel tip
positions. With PIE stopped, Send-Editor.py Scripts/Configure-RobotGameplay.py
updates the six pawn Blueprints and shared assets, adds missing level actors,
and saves the traversal map. Existing robot actor transforms are preserved.
Edit CrowdAgent patrol/run speed, home area and tether settings in the editor;
armor, damage, laser range and model-space muzzle points are pawn defaults.
Fit-RobotMuzzles.py runs in Blender to refit barrel tips against source geometry
and inspected orthographic aperture centres. It includes Vanguard's asymmetric
guns and all four industrial barrels; it does not change the model geometry.

Test-RobotGameplaySuite.py runs standalone robot checks, human crowd regression
and two-player checks, then restores editor quality and Play settings.
Test-RobotGameplay.py exercises actual player fire, peaceful sensing, gait,
retaliation, civilian flight, cover, disengagement and shutdown/respawn in PIE.
Test-RobotGameplay-Network.py exercises the same authoritative combat path
from a client in two-player listen-server PIE. Test-RobotMountedWeapons.py
checks real player provocation and the final fitted gun layout on each model.
Validate-RobotGameplayAssets.py checks the six Blueprints, their defaults and
saved traversal placements with PIE stopped. Runtime test fixtures are not
saved to the map. Results are in Artifacts/RobotGameplay and summarized in
resources/RobotGameplayValidation.json.

Edit in Blender
---------------
Art/Robots/<Name>/<Name>.blend is the editable rig. Source/ preserves the
original FBX and four textures, renamed to <Name>.fbx and
<Name>_BaseColor/Normal/Metallic/Roughness.png. SourceManifest.json records the
original archive/member names and hashes. Renamed archives are in mockups/Robots.

Select Armature and enter Pose Mode. The leg bones rotate around local X only;
their other axes and scales are locked. FootIK on the armature switches between
FK (0, the default) and the three-link foot solver (1). Move foot_control_l/r
and use knee_pole_l/r for the optional IK setup. The hydraulic aim constraints
orient rigid rods and housings; they never stretch those meshes.

Blender controls and constraints are authoring controls. Their evaluated motion
is baked into the FBX animation; they are not a live Unreal Control Rig.
When authoring another clip, keep joint angles within the visible armor's
clearance. Mechanical hinge limits do not provide armor collision detection.

Exports and scale
-----------------
Export/<Name>.fbx contains the complete rigidly weighted robot and skeleton.
Export/<Name>_JointCheck.fbx also contains the baked 30 fps test clip.
Export/Parts/*.fbx contains the individual modules without skeletons.
The delivered source height is 180 cm with unit armature/mesh/bone scale.

LOD0 retains the textured prototype detail and closed inner surfaces. Unreal
also generates LOD1 and LOD2 at approximately 50% and 25% of its triangles.
DA_RobotLODSettings preserves bone boundaries and limits weights to one bone.
These detailed mockup meshes still need profiling and a production topology
pass before using large crowds of robots; see validation for actual counts.

Rebuild and validate
--------------------
From the repository root:
  python Scripts/Prepare-RobotSources.py
  python Scripts/Prepare-RobotRigs.py

Prepare-RobotRigs.py accepts optional robot names. It imports and reduces a
working copy if its cache is missing, constructs fitted rigid modules, exports,
checks every vertex weight and edge length in nine poses, exercises foot IK,
checks piston alignment and reimports the delivered FBX. Originals stay intact.

With the project's editor bridge active and PIE stopped:
  python Scripts/Send-Editor.py Scripts/Import-RobotAssets.py
  python Scripts/Send-Editor.py Scripts/Validate-RobotImports.py

Capture-RobotPreviews.py can also be sent through the bridge. It starts a
temporary PIE session, loads full texture detail, captures neutral/bent poses,
checks that each foot actually moves in Unreal, then ends PIE and restores the
editor camera. Run Validate-RobotImports.py afterward to include these playback
results in the durable validation report. Captures are under Artifacts/Robots.

Set CR_EDITOR_TIMEOUT to 1800 for the full batch. ROBOT_IMPORT_NAMES in the
editor bridge globals can restrict an import; remove that override to import
all six. Imports save only their own assets, not the open traversal map.

resources/RobotRigValidation.json records Blender results and independent
Unreal checks of assets, sampled LOD skin weights/triangle bindings, animation
movement and unit bone scales. Artifacts/Robots holds local build logs and
rendered pose inspections. Generated binary assets use existing Git LFS rules.
